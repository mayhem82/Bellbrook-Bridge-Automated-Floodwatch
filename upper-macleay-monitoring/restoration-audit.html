UPPER MACLEAY FLOOD MONITORING

Historical Closure & Access-Restoration Audit

Evidence of the interval between falling floodwater and actual restoration of road access. Prepared as an extension to the Snapshot 012 discovery baseline for the 30 October 2026 submission.

Verified operational rule: Kempsey Shire Council states that after a flood a bridge will not be reopened until it has been inspected and debris has been cleared by Council staff. Therefore water level and access state are separate variables.

Measurement rule: exact hydrological-clearance-to-reopening duration is recorded only where the available publications establish both endpoints. Where they do not, this audit records a bounded interval or leaves the duration UNKNOWN.

Verified event evidence

26–28 MARCH 2021

Flood warning ended while bridges remained closed

By 7pm on 26 March Council reported river levels below minor flood levels and no current flood warning. Turners Flat was open, while Sherwood, Bellbrook, Temagog and Toorooka remained closed and Council said bridges were being inspected as river levels fell before being declared open. By 28 March all low-level Macleay bridges were open, while Dowlings Falls and Moparrabah causeways remained closed.

Finding: this event directly demonstrates a post-warning/post-hydrological phase in which access remained unavailable pending inspection and restoration activity.

Council: Macleay Valley flood update #50, 26 March 2021; Roads and bridges affected by flooding, 28 March 2021.

APRIL 2021

Dowlings Falls — restoration extended well beyond the river event

Council reported on 22 April that cleaning of Dowlings Falls Causeway had commenced on 19 April. A substantial amount of material deposited from upstream inhibited water flow and prohibited access, and pilot channels were required to make passage possible.

Finding: Dowlings Falls demonstrates that debris deposition and physical restoration can dominate access recovery after the hydrological event itself.

Council: Council teams still hard at work with flood repairs, 22 April 2021.

MARCH–APRIL 2022

Main Macleay crossings restored; Dowlings Falls remained closed

On 22 March Council reported Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog, Turners Flat, Sherwood and Dungay Creek open, while Dowlings Falls remained closed because repair works were required. On 4 April Council again reported all upstream Macleay bridges open and Dowlings Falls still closed.

Finding: a crossing can remain unavailable after the wider bridge network has returned to service because structural repair is a separate restoration pathway.

Council: Flood-impact Road and Bridge Update, 22 March 2022; Open and closed roads and bridges, 4 April 2022.

6–10 JULY 2022

Inspection-driven staged reopening

All upstream bridges were reported closed on 6 July. Council subsequently recorded that staff inspected and opened Sherwood and Toorooka at 2pm on 9 July, then inspected and opened Dungay Creek Causeway at 6pm. By noon on 10 July all upstream bridges were open. Dowlings Falls remained closed and Council later said it could remain closed for up to a week while water dropped enough for repairs.

Finding: this is direct evidence of sequential inspection and reopening rather than automatic reopening when floodwater falls.

Council: Flood event July 2022; Road and bridge closures following flood, updates 9–13 July 2022.

APRIL 2025

Dowlings Falls — flood-damage closure after other crossings reopened

At 11am on 29 April Council reported Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog, Turners Flat, Sherwood and Dungay Creek open. Dowlings Falls remained closed due to flood damage.

Council: Macleay River Rise, 29 April 2025.

MAY 2025

Structural damage outlasted the general crossing closures

On 29 May Council reported the principal Bellbrook–Sherwood crossings and Dungay Creek open while Dowlings Falls remained closed because the structure had been significantly damaged. The same update recorded other road/bridge damage including McCoy's Bridge and Majors Bridge closures.

Finding: restoration status must include damage and repair state, not only water height.

Council: Kempsey Shire Flood update #24, 29 May 2025.

5 AUGUST 2025

Final flood warning issued; four principal bridges still closed

Council reported that the final Macleay flood warning had been issued at 6:02am and that no further flooding was anticipated. Turners Flat and Dungay Creek were open, but Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog and Sherwood remained closed. Council expressly asked residents to allow time for staff to inspect and clean structures before opening them.

Finding: this is a particularly clear demonstration that the end of the flood-warning phase does not equal restoration of access.

Council: Macleay River Flood Update #6 and Roads Update, 5 August 2025.

Cross-event findings

  1. Hydrological state and access state are different. Council's own publications repeatedly preserve closures after water levels fall or formal flood warnings end.
  2. Inspection is a decision point. 2021, 2022 and 2025 publications explicitly identify inspection before reopening.
  3. Cleaning/debris removal is a decision point. Council's current camera page states debris must be cleared, and the 2021 Dowlings Falls record shows the scale this can reach.
  4. Damage creates a second restoration pathway. Dowlings Falls repeatedly remained closed after other crossings reopened because repair was required.
  5. Reopening is staged across the network. July 2022 records specific bridges being inspected/opened at different times.
  6. Flood-warning cessation is not an access-restoration timestamp. March 2021 and August 2025 demonstrate this directly.

Evidence gaps to request from Council

The public record proves the existence of the restoration interval but does not consistently expose all operational timestamps. The submission should request the underlying records necessary to quantify it:

  • time each bridge deck became hydrologically clear;
  • time inspection was requested, allocated and completed;
  • inspection outcome and defects identified;
  • time debris clearance commenced and finished;
  • engineering assessment/escalation where required;
  • repair commencement/completion where required;
  • identity/role authorised to reopen the crossing;
  • time reopening was authorised;
  • time public OPEN status was published;
  • priority rules where multiple isolated communities require inspection simultaneously;
  • whether medical, emergency-service, single-access or High Flood Island consequences affect priority.

Required metric: HYDROLOGICAL CLEARANCE → OFFICIAL REOPENING. The current public record supports the existence of this latency but does not provide enough paired timestamps to calculate it consistently for every crossing/event.
