UPPER MACLEAY FLOOD-CROSSING MONITORING GAP CATALOGUE

Snapshot Evidence 001–012

The substantive discovery chain behind the 30 October 2026 Floodwatch submission. These are evidence-bearing snapshot records, not placeholder references.

Continuity: each snapshot preserves the preceding record and appends corrections or new evidence. Snapshot 012 freezes the discovery baseline. Later structural-harm, access-restoration and risk-matrix work extends the frozen baseline without rewriting it.

SNAPSHOT 001

Baseline Discovery

Scope: identify flood-affected Upper Macleay bridges, causeways and road crossings; record gauges, cameras and public data; distinguish verified monitoring from unresolved gaps. Bellbrook Floodwatch is already operational and is treated as demonstrated capability.

  • Public cameras verified at Bellbrook Bridge, Turners Flat Bridge, Sherwood Bridge and Kempsey Traffic Bridge.
  • Public upriver gauges verified at Georges Creek, Bellbrook, Toorooka, Moparrabah, Turners Flat, Aldavilla and Sherwood Bridge.
  • Initial flood-relevant crossing set: Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog, Turners Flat, Sherwood, Dungay Creek, Dowlings Falls, Nurse Kirkpatrick, McCoy's and Powick's.
  • Nulla Nulla Creek Road identified as a multi-crossing corridor: Dunbars Crossing, Nurse Kirkpatrick, O'Neills, with historical Yellow Gully and McIntyres bridge works also identified.
  • Baseline rule: absence from Council's public gauge/camera pages is not proof that no sensor exists.

Primary source IDs established: S001–S009.

SNAPSHOT 002

Crossing/Gauge Relationship Audit

  • SES 2023 plan verifies Temagog Bridge has no gauge. Its condition is inferred from Toorooka Bridge Gauge observations, with Bellbrook also used in the documented relationship.
  • Direct monitoring confirmed for Bellbrook, Toorooka, Turners Flat and Sherwood.
  • Council event records confirm the recurrent seven-crossing operational set: Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog, Turners Flat, Sherwood, Dungay Creek and Dowlings Falls.
  • Tributary inventory expanded: Nurse Kirkpatrick flood damage, McCoy's structural damage, and multiple Nulla Nulla, Hickeys Creek and Willi Willi bridge assets.
  • Council's Recovery Action Plan already records an action to upgrade or replace flood gauges as deemed necessary. Gauge assessment is therefore an existing Council flood-readiness function, not a new function proposed by Floodwatch.

Snapshot additions/confirmations: S010–S016.

SNAPSHOT 003

Tributary & Isolation-Crossing Expansion

  • Dungay Creek Causeway had repeated damage/closure and was subject to more than $3 million betterment funding for a higher 75 m concrete bridge. Monitoring at the replacement crossing remained to be verified.
  • Dowlings Falls demonstrated persistent vulnerability: closed after other crossings reopened in 2021 and 2022, and significantly flood-damaged again in 2025.
  • Mungay Creek, Hickeys Creek, Willi Willi and Moparrabah were verified as tributary areas where low-lying bridges were affected.
  • Powick's Bridge, McCoy's Bridge and Nurse Kirkpatrick provided specific closure/damage evidence.
  • The catalogue was formally separated into Layer A — routinely reported Macleay crossings — and Layer B — tributary/local isolation crossings. Layer B was not treated as less important.

Snapshot additions: S013–S020.

SNAPSHOT 004

Monitoring-Network Reconciliation

  • Current Council baseline fixed at seven upriver gauge locations: Georges Creek, Bellbrook, Toorooka, Moparrabah, Turners Flat, Aldavilla and Sherwood Bridge.
  • Moparrabah corrected: it is a monitored tributary location. The unresolved issue is the exact hydraulic relationship between the Moparrabah/Majors Creek station and Majors Bridge/Parrabel Creek.
  • Historical Wittitrin/Dungay gauge evidence identified, but current public status was unresolved.
  • Dowlings Falls remained a verified flood-vulnerable crossing with no dedicated public monitoring identified in the current headline network.
  • Five monitoring arrangements emerged: direct monitoring; nearby/catchment monitoring; explicitly indirect bridge estimation; historical/uncertain monitoring; and no dedicated public monitoring identified.

Snapshot additions: S021–S025.

SNAPSHOT 005

Hidden/Historical Gauge Infrastructure Audit

  • Major discovery: historical SES records identify Kogal on Nulla Nulla Creek, Australian Water Resources Council (AWRC) 206900, then shown with Bureau of Meteorology (BOM) number 059036.
  • The 2023 flood plan still expresses the Nulla Creek Bridge/Thungutti isolation relationship against the Bellbrook gauge, despite historical Kogal infrastructure.
  • Wittitrin station 206903 / 059125 on Dungay Creek was confirmed, including a Council repair program after flood and bushfire damage.
  • Moparrabah station identity 206904 / 059128 on Parrabel Creek was strengthened.
  • No equivalent named river-height gauge was located in this pass for Hickeys Creek/Powick's, Willi Willi/McCoy's, Mungay Creek or Dowlings Falls.
  • Accountability question created: what became of historical/local monitoring sites, what capability remains, and what present data is publicly exposed?

Snapshot additions: S026–S030.

SNAPSHOT 006

Current-Network Evidence & Station Reconciliation

  • A 2023 catchment-resilience inventory identifies Kogal as an AUTOMATIC station, AWRC 206900, operated by Kempsey Shire Council, with BOM number 059056.
  • Wittitrin 059125 / 206903 is also identified as an AUTOMATIC station, operated by WaterNSW.
  • Critical distinction established: Dungay Creek/Wittitrin Causeway is described as ungauged even though a Dungay Creek station exists. Therefore STATION EXISTS and CROSSING IS DIRECTLY GAUGED are separate propositions.
  • Nulla Creek Bridge on Armidale Road is tied to Bellbrook gauge thresholds; Kogal's existence does not prove that Kogal directly gauges that bridge.
  • Revised taxonomy: direct crossing gauge/camera; catchment/tributary station; indirect crossing relationship; ungauged crossing within a monitored tributary; no named station identified.

Snapshot additions: S031–S034.

SNAPSHOT 007

Kogal Reconciliation & Critical-Access Expansion

  • Later BOM evidence resolves Kogal's modern identifier as 059056 / AWRC 206900, Kempsey Shire Council, Automatic, LOCAL service, High priority. The older 059036 entry is retained as identifier history rather than assumed to be a separate gauge.
  • No Bellbrook-equivalent public real-time river-height page or calibrated Kogal-stage relationship for the separate Nulla Nulla Creek Road bridges was established.
  • Dunbars Crossing was identified in Council asset-management evidence as a single point of access and therefore a critical asset.
  • Nurse Kirkpatrick had been replaced 1.5 m higher yet subsequently suffered flood-related abutment damage. Structural betterment and monitoring are therefore separate controls.
  • Candidate gap classes established: public-data exposure gap; crossing-calibration gap; spatial coverage gap; instrumentation-gap candidate; resilience-without-monitoring gap.
  • Correct Nulla Nulla request hardened: establish what Kogal measures, what it exposes publicly, which crossings it can validly represent, and only then determine additional monitoring needs.

Snapshot additions: S035–S041.

SNAPSHOT 008

Critical-Access Network & Monitoring-Gap Consolidation

  • Hickeys Creek/Powick's: verified flood-affected corridor; no dedicated public crossing monitoring identified in authoritative gauge material searched.
  • Willi Willi: multi-crossing corridor with documented interruption at McCoy's and Toorumbee plus six other named bridge assets; no dedicated local flood-warning station verified.
  • Mungay Creek: low-lying bridge impacts and Scotts Bridge asset confirmed; no named local flood-warning station verified.
  • Dowlings Falls multi-event pattern strengthened across 2021, 2022 and 2025, including debris/material deposition, repairs and significant damage.
  • Network hierarchy consolidated: established direct/public monitoring; station exists but crossing representation needs proof; documented indirect/ungauged relationships; strong candidate local-monitoring gaps.
  • Operational significance expanded: bridge inspection before reopening and uninspected-road uncertainty make persistent local information relevant to situational awareness and prioritisation, not just forecasting.

Snapshot additions: S042–S051.

SNAPSHOT 009

Isolation Consequence & Consolidated Register Baseline

  • Major finding: the Kempsey Shire Local Flood Plan explicitly states there is no appropriate flood-warning gauge for Willawarrin. Willawarrin is a High Flood Island.
  • Planning estimates show potentially prolonged isolation: Bellbrook/surrounding area 1–10 days in minor, 10–16 days in moderate and more than 20 days in major flooding; Willawarrin 5–7, 10–14 and more than 20 days respectively. These are planning estimates, not event forecasts.
  • Dungay Creek Road, Moparrabah Road and Willi Willi Road are formally related to an indicative Toorooka gauge relationship rather than dedicated local road/crossing gauges.
  • The bridge table confirms heterogeneous arrangements: direct, indirect and ungauged.
  • Consolidated register baseline created for Bellbrook, Toorooka, Temagog, Turners Flat, Sherwood, Dungay/Wittitrin, Dowlings Falls, Nulla Creek Bridge, Nulla Nulla Creek Road, Willawarrin/Hickeys, Willi Willi, Moparrabah/Majors and Mungay Creek.
  • Implementation logic: consider isolation consequence/no alternate route, repeated closure or structural damage, and weakness of local monitoring relationship.

Snapshot additions: S052–S055.

SNAPSHOT 010

Omitted Access Corridors & Implementation-Matrix Baseline

  • SES prepare-to-isolate warning footprint expands scope to Armidale Road, Five Day Creek Road, Nulla Nulla Creek Road, McIntyres Road, Hickeys Creek Road, Rossiters Road, Toose Road and Mackenzies Creek Road.
  • Five Day Creek Road has multiple bridge assets and formal isolation relevance; no dedicated local flood-warning station was verified.
  • Toose Road landslip caused severe isolation; approximately $21 million was committed to a permanent solution, including a future Macleay crossing near Jimmys Point. Monitoring should be designed into that new critical asset.
  • Armidale Road is the principal supply route and a landslip/flood resilience corridor, not merely a route between gauges. Fourteen landslip sites were recorded in the then-current repair program.
  • Georges Creek retained as an upstream High Flood Island/community node and operational upstream gauge input.
  • Implementation principle fixed: no blanket gauge installation — expose existing stations, calibrate where possible, add cameras where useful, install new monitoring where evidence supports it, and design monitoring into new infrastructure.

Snapshot additions: S056–S062.

SNAPSHOT 011

Peripheral Isolation Corridors & Matrix Hardening

  • McIntyres Bridge on Nulla Nulla Creek Road was distinguished from McIntyres Road. Shared names must not be merged without authoritative mapping.
  • Nulla Nulla Creek Road inventory expanded to at least five separately named assets: Dunbars, Nurse Kirkpatrick, O'Neills, McIntyres Bridge and Bung Hole Bridge.
  • Rossiters Road: verified isolation-warning corridor; crossing mechanism and monitoring source unresolved.
  • Mackenzies Creek Road: verified isolation-warning corridor; local crossing and monitoring infrastructure unresolved.
  • McIntyres Road: verified isolation-warning corridor; flood-crossing mechanism unresolved and kept separate from McIntyres Bridge.
  • Matrix-hardening rules established: source-backed consequence required; station existence separated from public accessibility and crossing calibration; monitoring absence stated cautiously; bridge betterment does not eliminate monitoring need; names are not merged by inference.
  • Three unresolved classes fixed: formal warning-gauge deficiency; known infrastructure with unclear coverage/public use; and formally warned isolation roads with unresolved failure mechanism/source.

Snapshot additions: S063–S068.

SNAPSHOT 012

Omission Check, Current Council Context & Discovery Freeze

  • Final omission check found no stronger additional Upper Macleay monitoring-gap corridor than the network already catalogued. Georges Creek, Bellbrook/surrounding area and Willawarrin remain the principal High Flood Island communities in the SES plan.
  • September 2026 secondary reporting of the Upper Macleay Flood Study indicated prolonged road inundation including Hickeys Creek Road up to 147.5 hours and Mungay Creek Road/Mines Road over 130 hours in the reported extreme/1% AEP scenario. These figures remain provisional until the primary study/minutes are obtained.
  • The same secondary report said Council resolved to seek funding to upgrade flood-gauge infrastructure throughout the Macleay catchment and reported a statement that a Nulla Nulla Creek gauge had never worked. This remains provisional pending primary Council records.
  • Primary Council annual reports independently verify strategic flood-warning monitoring review, new/improved gauges, Wittitrin/Bellbrook improvements, gauge-review expenditure and flood-mitigation monitoring-camera expenditure.
  • Frozen network: reference monitored nodes — Georges Creek, Bellbrook, Toorooka, Turners Flat, Sherwood; indirect/mixed — Temagog, Dungay/Wittitrin, Moparrabah/Majors, Nulla Creek Bridge/Armidale Road; multi-crossing Nulla Nulla corridor with unresolved Kogal coverage; verified warning-gauge deficiency — Willawarrin/Hickeys Creek; strong candidate gaps — Dowlings Falls, Willi Willi and Mungay Creek; additional assessment corridors — Five Day Creek, Toose/Jimmys Point, Rossiters, McIntyres Road, Mackenzies Creek Road and Armidale Road.
  • Mines Road retained as discovery reserve pending primary Flood Study evidence.
