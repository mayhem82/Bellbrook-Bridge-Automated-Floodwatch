INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE COMPANION

About this work

Background, authorship and purpose of the Upper Macleay Flood Monitoring Evidence Companion.

About the author

I am a Bellbrook resident and the independent developer of Bellbrook Floodwatch and this Upper Macleay Flood Monitoring Evidence Companion.

This work began from a practical local problem: people living in flood-prone Upper Macleay communities need clear, accessible information about the conditions affecting their access.

I developed Bellbrook Floodwatch independently as a working community situational-awareness system. From that work, I began examining the wider network of bridges, gauges, cameras, isolation routes and publicly available flood information across the Upper Macleay.

That investigation developed into the evidence catalogue presented here.

I am not presenting this work as an emergency authority, engineering authority or replacement for official warnings, bridge inspections or road-closure decisions. My role is independent: identify the available evidence, preserve its sources, identify information and monitoring gaps, and put practical questions and implementation options before the people and organisations with authority to act.

I live with the consequences of Upper Macleay isolation as part of the community I am examining. This project is therefore both evidence-driven and grounded in the practical experience of living in the area.

The evidence companion is independently maintained and may be shared with government representatives, Kempsey Shire Council, relevant agencies, community members and other interested parties.

Purpose and audience

This is a personal, independently authored evidence resource. It is not a publication of Kempsey Shire Council, the NSW Government, Michael Kemp MP (Member of Parliament) or any emergency-service authority.

The first formal use of the companion is to support the 30 October 2026 printed submission by an ordinary civilian and Bellbrook resident, acting as a constituent, to their elected NSW state representative, Michael Kemp MP. The purpose is to support better governance through better evidence, transparency, accountability and public administration. Michael Kemp is the first recipient, not the owner or identity of the project. Kempsey Shire Council and relevant agencies are subsequent implementation and accountability audiences.

Designed to be shared: after the initial submission, the same evidence companion can be provided to government representatives, Council, agencies, community members, media or other interested parties without changing its independent authorship.

Scope and boundaries

This companion examines publicly relevant evidence about Upper Macleay flood monitoring, access, isolation, post-flood restoration and the governance arrangements that affect those systems. It does not determine legal liability, engineering fitness, individual culpability or emergency operational decisions.

Method

Findings are developed from identifiable sources and are intended to remain traceable to the evidence supporting them. Verified facts, inference, questions and unresolved matters are kept distinct. Where the available evidence does not support a conclusion, the status remains UNKNOWN. Contrary evidence is retained and material corrections are made transparently rather than silently rewriting the investigation history.

Public-interest purpose

This work seeks improvement in systems, information, accountability and public administration. It is not designed to manufacture blame, target individuals or reach predetermined findings. Questions are directed toward establishing facts, records, responsibilities and practical opportunities for better governance.

Corrections and additional evidence

Government agencies, elected representatives, organisations and community members are invited to provide factual corrections, contrary evidence or additional records relevant to material published in this companion. A correction should identify the page or finding concerned, state the proposed correction and provide the supporting source or record where available.

Until a dedicated contact channel is published, a correction or evidence submission may be provided through the recipient or communication channel by which this companion was supplied. Substantive responses will be assessed against the same evidence standard and preserved transparently where they alter the record.

Version and currency

Material review date: 8 October 2026.

This is a maintained evidence companion. Source dates and snapshot history provide the authoritative chronology for individual findings; a later site review date does not silently replace or alter earlier evidence states.

Relationship to the printed submission

The printed submission is intended to remain understandable on its own. This website is its evidence companion: it carries the deeper investigation record, snapshots, matrices, structural-harm analysis, source material, definitions and supporting documentary evidence.

The website is therefore an audit trail and evidence-access layer, not a substitute for the printed submission.

Independence

Bellbrook Floodwatch already operates independently of Council. The wider evidence companion applies the same principle: use available evidence, identify what is verified, preserve uncertainty where evidence is incomplete, and distinguish community situational awareness from the legal and operational authority of government agencies.

No personal name or alias is published on this page.

Important disclaimer

Independent status. This is an independent community evidence and advocacy project. It is not operated by, endorsed by or affiliated with Kempsey Shire Council, NSW State Emergency Service (NSW SES), the Bureau of Meteorology, WaterNSW or any other government authority.

Not an emergency service. This site must not be relied upon for emergency warnings, evacuation decisions, road-closure authority or determining whether a flooded road, bridge or crossing is safe to use. Follow official emergency warnings, road closures and directions from the responsible authorities.

Evidence status. Findings distinguish verified evidence, inference, unresolved questions and UNKNOWN. Failure to identify evidence in the records examined is not represented as proof that the evidence or arrangement does not exist.

Currency. Government records, monitoring arrangements, road conditions and agency procedures can change. Findings should be read with their cited source and date.

No professional advice. Material is provided for public-interest research and advocacy. It is not legal, engineering, hydrological, medical or emergency-management advice.

Corrections and responses. Agencies and other affected parties may provide corrections, additional records or contrary evidence. Substantive corrections are to be preserved transparently rather than silently rewriting the evidence history.
