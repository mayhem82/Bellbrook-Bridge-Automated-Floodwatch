Bellbrook noticeboard: Upper Macleay Flood Study

Evidence provenance

Three investigator-supplied photographs of Kempsey Shire Council flood-study notices displayed on the Bellbrook community noticeboard, supplied in conversation on 12 October 2026. Photography date and posting date unverified. Original photographs are not hosted on this page. This record excludes unrelated noticeboard materials.

Stakeholder invitation

Council described the Upper Macleay Flood Study as technically completed and invited stakeholders to a session on Friday 16 October 2026, 1–3 pm, Willawarrin Community Hall, 22 Armidale Road. Study area: Macleay River and tributaries near Comara downstream towards Kempsey. The notice distinguishes the technical Flood Study from a subsequent Flood Risk Management Study and Plan. It says the study itself proposes no mitigation works, planning control changes or specific risk-management actions.

Presentation notice

WMAwater consultants and Kempsey Shire Council staff were advertised as presenters. The notice describes maps and visualisations of flood movement and refers to ksc.pub/UMFS.

Community flyer

Topics advertised include flood levels, extent, depth and behaviour across different events. The flyer requests community knowledge and experiences, and depicts data collection, Flood Study, Flood Risk Management Study and Flood Risk Management Plan as stages.

Evidence limits

These notices do not establish installation of crossing gauges, live access information, changed bridge reopening procedures, adoption of a risk-management plan or implementation of mitigation. Those matters remain UNKNOWN pending records.

Pre-submission accountability baseline — 12 October 2026

This section compares publicly documented government workstreams before the independent Floodwatch findings have been submitted. It does not assess any government response to those unsubmitted findings.

Flood modelling: Kempsey Shire Council states the WMAwater Upper Macleay Flood Study is technically complete but not yet adopted by Council. It models levels, depths, velocities, extents and flood development. Source: Your Say Macleay.

Flood-warning infrastructure: NSW's floodplain grant listing separately describes a Kempsey flood-warning action implementation project, including additional gauges, correlation of Kempsey and Smithtown gauges, and ENVIRONMON improvements. Completion and operational coverage are not established by the grant description. Source: NSW floodplain grants.

Flood policy: Kempsey Shire Council's 16 June 2026 meeting materials include a draft Flood Policy and exhibition/submission documents. This is a distinct governance process and not proof of adoption of the Upper Macleay Flood Study. Source: Council meeting record.

Timetable: WMAwater's public page anticipated a draft in May 2024; a Council project page referred to 2025 exhibition; the current consultation is in October 2026. These are published target changes, not proof of misconduct or contractual breach. Sources: WMAwater and Council project page.

Outstanding accountability questions: study assumptions and model validation; whether crossings are individually mapped; which gauges are live and accessible; responsible authorities for access and reopening decisions; implementation status of funded warning upgrades; and Council's eventual consideration of consultation submissions. Each requires its own primary records. No response failure can be attributed to Council for independent findings not yet submitted.

Primary-record extension — 12 October 2026

NSW funding programme: The NSW Government's floodplain grants awarded register describes two distinct Kempsey projects: the Upper (Central) Macleay flood study, intended to support flood knowledge and evacuation-route planning; and “Flood warning action implementation — Macleay River”, intended to review and add mid-catchment gauges, correlate Kempsey and Smithtown gauges, clarify active gauges in ENVIRONMON, and enhance that system. These are project descriptions, not verified completion certificates.

Historic delivery milestone: Council's 2022–26 delivery programme, action EN.OP66, specified installation of new rain and river gauges by June 2023. Achievement against that target is not established here.

Existing warning arrangements: The NSW SES Kempsey Shire Local Flood Plan contains monitoring arrangements and gauge inventories, including Bellbrook and Turners Flat rainfall monitoring. Rainfall monitoring must not be represented as a river-height gauge or bridge-crossing threshold without station-specific evidence.

Stage distinction: NSW's floodplain management grants framework distinguishes flood study, flood risk management study, investigation/design, and implementation. A technical study is not itself implementation.

Next primary records to obtain: the technical report and appendices; gauge procurement, commissioning and current station inventory; ENVIRONMON acceptance and public access documentation; EN.OP66 completion reporting; and records identifying which crossing closures and reopenings are supported by which gauges and responsible authorities. These are document requests, not established deficiencies.
