UPPER MACLEAY FLOOD MONITORING
AND ACCESS RESTORATION

Submission to Michael Kemp MP
30 October 2026

Prepared by an ordinary civilian and Bellbrook resident acting as a constituent.
Independent public-interest evidence and advocacy submission.

Purpose: to support better governance through better evidence, transparency, accountability and public administration.

1. Executive submission

This submission asks for a practical, evidence-led review of flood monitoring and post-flood access restoration across the Upper Macleay. The central problem is not simply whether gauges or cameras exist. It is whether residents and responsible authorities have sufficiently clear, crossing-specific information from flood threat through closure, inspection, debris clearance, repair, reopening authorisation and publication of restored access.

The evidence companion identifies locations with established controls, locations where existing monitoring may be usable but its relationship to a crossing requires validation, and locations where a genuine monitoring or public-information gap remains unresolved. It also identifies a separate post-flood access-restoration interval: water receding does not itself mean a road or bridge is open.

The requested response is targeted: establish what monitoring source is relied upon for each critical crossing, expose or validate suitable existing data before duplicating infrastructure, assess new monitoring only where a real coverage deficiency is established, and make the post-flood inspection and reopening process auditable.

2. Core findings

Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek

Official flood planning identifies Willawarrin as a High Flood Island and records that there is no appropriate flood-warning gauge for the area. This is the clearest identified warning-monitoring deficiency and is classified CRITICAL in the companion.

Nulla Nulla / Kogal

Kogal AWRC 206900 is documented as an automatic station associated with Nulla Nulla Creek. The unresolved issue is which individual crossings it can validly represent and whether usable operational data is publicly accessible. Station existence must not be treated as proof that every crossing is directly gauged.

Dowlings Falls

Repeated closure, repair and significant flood damage are documented. No dedicated public monitoring was identified in the audited network. The location also demonstrates that structural damage can extend isolation after floodwater has receded.

Bellbrook and principal monitored crossings

Bellbrook, Turners Flat and Sherwood have comparatively strong monitoring controls. Their residual governance gap is not simply hydrology: authoritative closure, inspection, cleaning, repair and reopening status must remain distinct from a water-level reading.

3. Structural harm identified

Fragmented monitoring and access information can create an incomplete situational picture; transfer interpretation burden to residents; increase ambiguity and information latency; and leave isolation consequences poorly understood. Identified mechanisms include source fragmentation, crossing-to-gauge ambiguity, false completeness from station existence, separation of hydrology and authoritative road status, cognitive-load transfer, single-access consequence amplification, medical/essential-service isolation, post-warning information gaps, inspection/debris sequencing, damage-dependent reopening and missing restoration-time metrics.

4. Access restoration is a separate operational phase

NORMAL → FLOOD THREAT → WATER OVER CROSSING / CLOSED → WATER RECEDING → WATER CLEAR BUT CLOSED → AWAITING INSPECTION → INSPECTED / AWAITING DEBRIS CLEARANCE → DAMAGE / REPAIR REQUIRED (where applicable) → ACCESS RESTORED → OPEN STATUS PUBLISHED.

Council publications from 2021, 2022 and 2025 demonstrate that crossings can remain closed after flood warnings end or water levels fall. The required performance metric is HYDROLOGICAL CLEARANCE → OFFICIAL REOPENING.

5. Evidence-based priority

CRITICAL: Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek; Dowlings Falls Causeway.

HIGH: Nulla Nulla Creek Road corridor; Toorooka Bridge; Temagog Bridge; Dungay Creek / Wittitrin; Willi Willi corridor; Mungay Creek Road; Five Day Creek Road; Armidale Road corridor.

ESTABLISHED CONTROLS: Bellbrook Bridge; Turners Flat Bridge; Sherwood Bridge; Georges Creek.

MODERATE: Moparrabah / Majors Bridge; Toose Road / Jimmys Point.

UNKNOWN: Rossiters Road; McIntyres Road; Mackenzies Creek Road, pending sufficient evidence.

6. Requested actions

  1. Identify the monitoring source relied upon for each flood-critical crossing/corridor and whether the relationship is direct, calibrated, indirect, ungauged or unresolved.
  2. Publish suitable existing data and validate station-to-crossing relationships before duplicating hardware.
  3. Assess new gauge and/or camera infrastructure where adequate local monitoring is not established.
  4. Provide the documented post-flood bridge inspection, debris-clearance, repair and reopening procedure, including delegations and prioritisation criteria.
  5. Measure hydrological-clearance-to-official-reopening time for critical crossings.
  6. Give explicit weight to High Flood Island communities, single-access roads, prolonged isolation, medical-service consequences and repeatedly damaged crossings.
  7. Check connecting-road status before treating bridge reopening as full access restoration.
  8. Identify responsible agencies, funding pathways, next steps and indicative implementation timeframes.
  9. Provide or facilitate access to outstanding records in the Information Requests register.

7. Decision points requiring clarification

  • Who owns and operates each monitoring asset relied upon for each critical crossing?
  • Who allocates post-flood inspections and what prioritisation rules apply?
  • What inspection standard or work instruction governs reopening?
  • What defect threshold triggers engineering escalation?
  • Who is delegated to authorise reopening?
  • What record captures the reopening decision and timestamp?
  • Who publishes OPEN status and what publication target applies?
  • How do single-access isolation, medical needs, emergency-service access and High Flood Island status affect priority?

10. Evidence and engagement standard

The submission does not assign culpability where the evidence does not support it. Verified facts, inference, questions and unresolved matters remain distinct. UNKNOWN remains UNKNOWN. Contrary evidence and substantive corrections are retained transparently. Information requests seek operational facts and records, not admissions.

11. Requested response

I ask that this submission and its evidence companion be considered as a practical governance and infrastructure-information issue affecting Upper Macleay communities. I request a written response addressing the requested actions, identifying which matters can be resolved from existing records, which require Council or agency clarification, and which warrant technical or infrastructure assessment.

Where responsibility sits with another authority, I ask that the relevant matter be identified and referred or that the responsible body be clearly named so the evidence request can be directed correctly.
