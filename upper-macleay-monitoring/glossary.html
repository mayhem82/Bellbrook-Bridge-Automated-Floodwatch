UPPER MACLEAY FLOOD MONITORING

Master Glossary

Controlled definitions for terminology used across the evidence catalogue, snapshots, structural-harm analysis, risk matrix and implementation matrix.

Interpretation rule: these definitions control how terms are used on this evidence site. A monitoring station existing in a catchment does not, by itself, mean a particular bridge is directly gauged or that its data is publicly usable.

ABCDEFGHIKMPRSUW

Access restoration

Return of usable access after a flood closure. It may occur later than hydrological clearance because inspection, debris removal, damage assessment, repair and reopening authorisation can intervene.

BOM (Bureau of Meteorology)

Australia’s national weather agency. BOM identifiers and flood/river information are used when identifying or reconciling monitoring stations and official hydrological information.

CE (Companion Evidence)

The CE-## identifier used by this companion site to index evidence components supporting the printed advocacy package.

DOC (Document)

The DOC-### identifier used in the Official Documents & Evidence Library for an authoritative or evidentiary source record.

MP (Member of Parliament)

An elected member of a parliament. In this package, Michael Kemp MP is the first formal recipient; the title does not imply ownership or control of the evidence.

NSW (New South Wales)

The Australian state in which the Upper Macleay investigation area is located.

SES (State Emergency Service)

NSW State Emergency Service, the emergency service responsible for flood, storm and tsunami response in New South Wales and a primary source of flood-planning and preparedness material used in this evidence set.

URL (Uniform Resource Locator)

The web address used to locate an online source or resource.

Access-restoration latency

The elapsed time between hydrological clearance of a crossing and official restoration/reopening of access.

Access state

The operational condition of a crossing or corridor, such as open, flood threatened, closed, water clear but closed, awaiting inspection, awaiting debris clearance, repair required or reopened.

Automatic station

A monitoring station that records or transmits observations automatically. Automatic does not necessarily mean its data is public, real-time, crossing-specific or suitable for warning decisions.

AEP (Annual Exceedance Probability)

A flood-probability expression: the probability that a specified flood level or magnitude will be equalled or exceeded in any given year. For example, 1% AEP means a 1% chance in any year; it is not a prediction that flooding occurs only once every 100 years.

AWRC (Australian Water Resources Council) number

Australian Water Resources Council station identifier used to distinguish monitoring sites. Historical and modern identifiers must be reconciled before treating similarly named records as separate stations.

Betterment

Reconstruction or replacement intended to improve resilience beyond simply restoring the damaged asset to its previous condition. Betterment of a bridge does not automatically eliminate a monitoring or information gap.

Bridge reopening

The authoritative decision that a previously closed bridge may again be used. Falling water or a visibly clear deck is not treated as equivalent to official reopening.

Calibration / validated relationship

An evidence-supported relationship between a monitoring observation and conditions at a particular crossing. A nearby or upstream gauge is not assumed to represent a bridge without such a relationship.

Camera monitoring

Visual observation of a crossing or river location. Cameras can show deck inundation, debris and visible conditions but do not replace calibrated water-level measurements or authoritative inspection.

Catchment / tributary station

A monitoring station located within the relevant creek or river system. Its existence does not establish that every crossing in that catchment is directly gauged.

Critical access

A route, bridge or crossing whose loss can materially isolate residents or interrupt essential movement, supplies or services.

Dedicated monitoring

Monitoring specifically associated with the crossing or location being assessed, rather than an indirect or remote catchment relationship.

Direct crossing monitoring

A gauge, camera or other observation system whose measurement location directly represents the crossing under assessment.

Discovery baseline

The preserved body of evidence and classifications reached at a specified snapshot. Snapshot 012 is the frozen discovery baseline for this investigation.

Established controls

Risk-matrix classification indicating substantial monitoring or information controls already exist. It does not mean the crossing has zero residual risk.

Evidence-based risk classification

Qualitative classification based on documented exposure, access consequence, information/control weakness and medical/essential-service consequence. It is not a manufactured numerical probability score.

Floodwatch

The independently operating Bellbrook community situational-awareness system that combines available observations and history. It does not replace official warnings, road closures, inspections or reopening decisions.

Fragmented information

Relevant flood, gauge, camera, road, warning or access-restoration information distributed across separate sources in a way that requires users to reconcile it themselves.

Gauge

A station or instrument used to observe water level or related hydrological conditions. On this site, “gauge exists” is deliberately separated from “crossing is directly gauged.”

Gauge deficiency

A documented inadequacy in available flood-warning gauge coverage. The Willawarrin finding is treated separately because the official flood plan states there is no appropriate flood-warning gauge for the area.

High Flood Island

A flood-isolation classification used in emergency planning for an area that becomes isolated by floodwater while remaining above the relevant flood level at the occupied area. Consequence and duration must be taken from the applicable planning evidence.

Hydrological clearance

The point at which floodwater has receded sufficiently from a crossing for the water condition itself no longer to be the immediate obstruction. It is not equivalent to official reopening.

Indirect monitoring

A crossing condition inferred from another gauge or observation point rather than measured directly at that crossing.

Information latency

Delay between a material change in real-world conditions and that change becoming available to the person who needs to make or understand an access decision.

Isolation consequence

The effects of loss of access, including interruption to normal travel, supplies, healthcare, carers, work, education or emergency movement. Consequence varies by location and event.

Kogal

The documented automatic monitoring station on Nulla Nulla Creek, AWRC 206900. Its existence is established; the investigation separately asks what it measures, what data is publicly available and which individual crossings it can validly represent.

Medical / essential-service consequence

Potential interruption to normal access to medication, appointments, wound care, carers, essential supplies or emergency movement during isolation. Emergency resupply is a mitigation, not restoration of normal access.

Monitoring gap

A deficiency in useful monitoring or information coverage. It may be an instrumentation gap, public-data gap, calibration gap, spatial coverage gap or restoration-state information gap.

Public-data exposure gap

A condition where monitoring infrastructure may exist but suitable observations are not readily available to the public for situational awareness.

Proxy relationship

Use of another gauge or observation point to infer conditions at a crossing. Proxy relationships should be documented and validated rather than assumed.

Residual risk

Risk remaining after existing controls are considered. At a well-monitored bridge this may shift from hydrological uncertainty toward inspection, debris clearance, damage and reopening latency.

Risk matrix

The crossing-by-crossing evidence framework assessing flood/access exposure, consequence of loss of access, information/control weakness, medical/essential-service consequence and required control. UNKNOWN is retained where evidence is insufficient.

Single-access route / single point of access

A route or crossing whose failure can remove the ordinary road access available to the affected residents or area.

Snapshot

A continuity record preserving the investigation state at a defined point. Each snapshot carries forward earlier evidence and records additions, corrections and unresolved questions.

SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) / work instruction

The documented operational process governing actions such as post-flood inspection, debris clearance, damage escalation, engineering assessment, reopening authority and publication of access status.

Station exists ≠ crossing directly gauged

Core evidence rule: the presence of a monitoring station in a creek or catchment cannot be used alone to claim that a particular bridge has direct monitoring.

Structural harm mechanism

A system-level pathway through which fragmented information, unclear responsibility, delayed restoration or weak monitoring can increase exposure or consequence without requiring a single individual error to be the sole cause.

Ungauged crossing

A crossing for which no direct gauge measurement is identified or where authoritative material describes the crossing as ungauged. This can coexist with a monitoring station elsewhere in the same tributary.

UNKNOWN

Evidence classification used where available material is insufficient to support a factual conclusion. UNKNOWN is not treated as zero risk, proof of absence or permission to infer the missing fact.

Warning-gauge deficiency

An identified weakness in gauge coverage relevant to flood warning. It is stronger than merely failing to find a public gauge because it depends on affirmative evidence that appropriate warning coverage is deficient.

Water clear but closed

Access state after floodwater has receded from the crossing but before the responsible authority has completed the required inspection, debris clearance, damage response and reopening decision.
