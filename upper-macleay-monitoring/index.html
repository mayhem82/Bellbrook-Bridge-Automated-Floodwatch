Scope and evidence discipline

The investigation asks what monitoring and access information actually serves flood-critical Upper Macleay crossings and isolation corridors. It does not assume every crossing requires a new gauge. Existing infrastructure is to be used, exposed or calibrated where adequate; new infrastructure follows evidence of a genuine coverage deficiency.

Classification rule: “No dedicated monitoring identified” means none was identified in the authoritative networks examined. It is not proof that no unlisted sensor exists. Comparative CRITICAL, HIGH and MODERATE ratings were withdrawn in submission v1.1 and remain withdrawn in v1.2. Consequences, monitoring controls and evidence gaps are recorded separately. Where evidence is insufficient the field remains UNKNOWN.

3 Documented conditions and analytical concerns

Documented conditions include differing monitoring arrangements, unresolved crossing relationships and event records of closure and damage. The following are analytical concerns to test against specific locations and events; they are not a declaration that every causal step or mechanism has been established. A complete mechanism coverage assessment is not certified by this submission.

Crossing-to-gauge relationships may be unclear even where a station is listed. Clarify each relationship before treating station existence as complete coverage.

A general monitoring inventory may conceal differences between catchment observations and direct crossing observations. Record both independently.

Hydrological information, images and authorised road status answer different questions. Ask how agencies reconcile and publish them.

Inference: fragmented information may increase residents’ interpretation burden. This submission does not quantify that burden or establish resulting individual decision errors.

Potential consequences to assess include interruption to medication, wound care, carers, essential supplies and emergency-service access. No individual medical outcome is established by this submission.

Event records justify inquiry into inspection, debris clearance and damage-dependent reopening. They do not establish that every elapsed interval was avoidable.

The reviewed submission material does not provide a complete event-level dataset measuring clearance, authorisation and public notification. This is a record-acquisition gap, not proof that agencies hold no such records.

4 Access restoration and proposed measurement

Proposed analytical model: record flood threat, protective closure, inundation, recession, inspection status, debris clearance, damage assessment, repair, reopening authorisation, physical access and public notification as distinct states or events. Closure can precede overtopping. Inspection, clearance and repair can overlap or recur. Restricted access and reclosure must be recorded. This model is a proposed reporting structure, not an asserted Council procedure.

Council’s 29 May 2025 update distinguishes reopened crossings from damaged Dowlings Falls and identifies continuing recovery work. It supports separating structural restoration from water-level information; it does not establish the start or duration of every restoration activity. [Appendix B, R03, River Crossings section.]

Proposed measures: (a) crossing-specific hydrological clearance to reopening authorisation; (b) authorisation to public OPEN notification; and (c) closure to usable corridor access, including connecting roads. Define hydrological clearance as the first documented post-inundation observation meeting an agency-specified local water criterion. Record the criterion, observer, method, time zone and uncertainty. It is not a safety clearance. If observations only bound the time, report the interval rather than inventing an exact timestamp. Where no inundation occurred or no defensible start can be established, record NOT APPLICABLE or UNKNOWN. Capture inspection allocation, attendance, clearance, engineering escalation and repair separately; assess reasons and resources before attributing avoidable delay.

5 Assessment priorities and evidence sufficiency

The earlier CRITICAL, HIGH and MODERATE labels are withdrawn from this successor version as comparative ratings because the reviewed submission did not supply a reproducible grading rule. The earlier version remains preserved. No location is removed or declared low risk by this correction.

Consequence: record closure, damage, isolation, single-access dependence and relevant planning classifications separately. Monitoring: record assets, operation, public access and crossing relationship separately. Evidence: distinguish documented source statements from unresolved present conditions. Priority: request agency assessment with reasons, without converting missing evidence into a low priority.

Proposed first-response focus: Willawarrin/Hickeys Creek for its documented planning deficiency; Dowlings Falls for repeated closure/damage; and Kogal/Nulla Nulla for unresolved public data and crossing coverage. This is an advocacy proposal grounded in those different issues, not a quantitative risk ranking or a finding that other communities have lower consequences.

The assessment must separately retain Nulla Creek Bridge on Armidale Road. It is not interchangeable with the multi-crossing Nulla Nulla Creek Road corridor. The incorporated plan records an indirect relationship for that bridge. [Snapshot 005, section 2, S027; Appendix B, R01, Volume 2 section 2.2.6 and Table 34.]

Rossiters Road, McIntyres Road and Mackenzies Creek Road remain in scope with crossing mechanisms and monitoring unresolved. McIntyres Road remains distinct from McIntyres Bridge on Nulla Nulla Creek Road. [Snapshot 011, sections 1–5.]

Appendix A supplies the complete 20-location assessment inventory. Existing controls do not establish low consequence, and an unresolved monitoring question does not establish absence of all monitoring.

6 Actions requested through Michael Kemp MP

1. Please ask the responsible agencies to identify the monitoring source relied upon for each flood-critical crossing or corridor and state whether the relationship is direct, calibrated, indirect, ungauged or unresolved.

2. Please ask the responsible agencies to publish suitable existing data and validate station-to-crossing relationships before duplicating monitoring hardware.

3. Please ask the responsible agencies to assess new gauge and/or camera infrastructure where adequate local monitoring is not established.

4. Please ask the responsible agencies to provide the documented post-flood bridge inspection, debris-clearance, repair and reopening procedure, including delegations and prioritisation criteria.

5. Please ask the responsible agencies to measure the defined restoration intervals in section 4 where adequate records exist; record missing timestamps, uncertainty, restrictions and reasons for delay.

6. Please ask the responsible agencies to give explicit weight to High Flood Island communities, single-access roads, prolonged isolation, medical-service consequences and repeatedly damaged crossings.

7. Please ask the responsible agencies to check connecting-road status before treating bridge reopening as full access restoration.

8. Please ask the responsible agencies to identify responsible agencies, funding pathways, next steps and indicative implementation timeframes.

Role of Bellbrook Floodwatch

Bellbrook Floodwatch is already independently operational. It is the demonstrated implementation, not a request for government permission or control. Official emergency warnings, road closures, bridge inspections and reopening decisions remain with the responsible authorities. The evidence-site extension distinguishes hydrological condition from authoritative access state.

Important disclaimer

Independent status. This evidence companion is an independent community evidence and advocacy resource. It is not operated by, endorsed by or affiliated with Kempsey Shire Council, NSW SES, the Bureau of Meteorology, WaterNSW or any other government authority.

Not an emergency service. Do not rely on this companion for emergency warnings, evacuation decisions, road-closure authority or deciding whether a flooded road, bridge or crossing is safe to use. Follow official warnings, closures and directions from responsible authorities.

Evidence and currency. Verified evidence, inference, unresolved questions and UNKNOWN are kept distinct. Failure to identify evidence is not proof that it does not exist. Records, monitoring arrangements, road conditions and procedures can change; findings should be read with their cited source and date.

No professional advice. Material is provided for public-interest research and advocacy and is not legal, engineering, hydrological, medical or emergency-management advice.

Corrections and responses. Corrections, additional records and contrary evidence are accepted and preserved transparently; substantive changes are not silently rewritten into the evidence history.
