Scope and evidence discipline

The investigation asks what monitoring and access information actually serves flood-critical Upper Macleay crossings and isolation corridors. It does not assume every crossing requires a new gauge. Existing infrastructure is to be used, exposed or calibrated where adequate; new infrastructure follows evidence of a genuine coverage deficiency.

Classification rule: “No dedicated monitoring identified” means none was identified in the authoritative networks examined. It is not proof that no unlisted sensor exists.

Core findings

Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek

Official flood planning identifies Willawarrin as a High Flood Island and states there is no appropriate flood-warning gauge for the area.

Nulla Nulla / Kogal

Kogal AWRC 206900 is documented as an automatic station associated with Nulla Nulla Creek. The unresolved question is which crossings it can validly represent and whether usable data is publicly available.

Dowlings Falls

Repeated closure, repair and significant flood damage are documented. No dedicated public monitoring was identified in the audited network.

Post-flood access

Water receding does not equal restored access. Council requires post-flood bridge inspection and debris clearance before reopening, and damage can extend closure after the hydrological event.

Structural harm — fragmented information

Mechanism chain: fragmented monitoring and access information → incomplete situational picture → interpretation burden transferred to residents → ambiguity and information latency → potential delayed or incorrect access decisions → prolonged or poorly understood isolation → increased exposure to medical, supply and access consequences.

The structural-harm analysis now contains 28 mechanisms covering source fragmentation, gauge ambiguity, public-access failure, cognitive load, single-access amplification, medical-service isolation, post-warning access gaps, inspection and debris-clearance sequencing, damage-dependent reopening, secondary route failure and missing restoration-time metrics.

Access restoration is a separate phase

NORMAL → FLOOD THREAT → WATER OVER CROSSING / CLOSED → WATER RECEDING → WATER CLEAR BUT CLOSED → AWAITING INSPECTION → INSPECTED / AWAITING DEBRIS CLEARANCE → DAMAGE / REPAIR REQUIRED if applicable → ACCESS RESTORED → OPEN STATUS PUBLISHED

For evidence purposes, the investigation should measure the interval from hydrological clearance to official reopening rather than assuming that a falling river ends isolation.

Medical and essential-service consequence

Isolation can interrupt ordinary access to medication, medical appointments, wound care and other services. NSW SES treats essential medication resupply and transport to medical appointments as recognised isolation needs. Emergency resupply mitigates the consequence; it is not equivalent to restored normal access.

Procedure / SOP accountability

The submission should request Council's current documented post-flood bridge-reopening procedure, SOP, work instruction or equivalent. Required fields include closure and inspection triggers, inspection responsibility, prioritisation criteria, debris clearance, damage escalation, engineering sign-off where required, reopening authority, publication of status, connecting-road checks and whether single-access or medically consequential isolation affects priority.

Requested implementation actions

  • Identify the monitoring source relied upon for each flood-critical crossing/corridor and whether the relationship is direct, calibrated, indirect, ungauged or unresolved.
  • Publish suitable existing data and validate station-to-crossing relationships before duplicating hardware.
  • Assess new gauge and/or camera infrastructure where adequate local monitoring is not established.
  • Publish or provide the documented post-flood bridge inspection, debris-clearance, repair and reopening process.
  • Measure hydrological-clearance-to-official-reopening time for critical crossings.
  • Give explicit weight to High Flood Island communities, single-access roads, prolonged isolation, medical-service consequences and repeatedly damaged crossings.
  • Check connecting-road status before treating bridge reopening as full access restoration.
  • Provide responsible agencies, funding pathways, next steps and indicative implementation timeframes.

Role of Bellbrook Floodwatch

Bellbrook Floodwatch is already independently operational. It is the demonstrated implementation, not a request for government permission or control. Official emergency warnings, road closures, bridge inspections and reopening decisions remain with the responsible authorities. The evidence-site extension distinguishes hydrological condition from authoritative access state.
