Upper Macleay Flood Monitoring

Crossing-by-crossing evidence catalogue, monitoring-gap investigation and implementation matrix developed from the Bellbrook Floodwatch investigation. This site is separate from the operational Bellbrook Floodwatch webapp.

Scope and evidence discipline

The investigation asks what monitoring actually serves flood-critical Upper Macleay crossings and isolation corridors. It does not assume every crossing requires a new gauge. Existing infrastructure is to be used, exposed or calibrated where adequate; new infrastructure follows evidence of a genuine coverage deficiency.

Classification rule: “No dedicated monitoring identified” means none was identified in the authoritative networks examined. It is not proof that no unlisted sensor exists.

Core findings

Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek

Official flood planning identifies Willawarrin as a High Flood Island and states there is no appropriate flood-warning gauge for the area. Powick’s Bridge/Hickeys Creek has documented flood impacts.

Nulla Nulla / Kogal

Kogal AWRC 206900 is documented as an automatic station associated with Nulla Nulla Creek. The corridor contains multiple separately identified crossings including Dunbars, Nurse Kirkpatrick, O’Neills, McIntyres and Bung Hole. The unresolved question is which crossings Kogal can validly represent and whether usable data is publicly available.

Dowlings Falls

Repeated closure, repair and significant flood damage are documented across multiple events. No dedicated public monitoring was identified in the audited network, making this a strong technical-assessment candidate.

Indirect and mixed monitoring

Temagog, Dungay/Wittitrin, Moparrabah/Majors and other corridors demonstrate that station existence is not the same as direct crossing monitoring. Remote relationships need to be documented and validated.

Other isolation corridors

Willi Willi, Mungay Creek, Five Day Creek, Rossiters Road, McIntyres Road, Mackenzies Creek Road and Armidale Road remain in the evidence catalogue at different levels of resolution. Toose Road/Jimmys Point is a prospective opportunity to design monitoring into new critical infrastructure.

Snapshot chain 001–012

The catalogue was developed as a continuity chain. Snapshot 012 freezes the discovery baseline for the 30 October 2026 submission while allowing explicit append-only corrections or stronger later evidence.

Requested implementation actions

  • Identify the monitoring source relied upon for each flood-critical crossing/corridor and whether the relationship is direct, calibrated, indirect, ungauged or unresolved.
  • Publish suitable data from existing infrastructure and validate station-to-crossing relationships before duplicating hardware.
  • Assess new gauge and/or camera infrastructure where an adequate local relationship is not established.
  • Give explicit weight to High Flood Island communities, single-access roads, prolonged isolation and repeatedly damaged crossings.
  • Consider monitoring and public-data requirements during delivery of new critical crossings.
  • Provide responsible agencies, funding pathways, next steps and indicative implementation timeframes.

Role of Bellbrook Floodwatch

Bellbrook Floodwatch is already independently operational. It is the demonstrated implementation, not a request for government permission or control. Official emergency warnings, road closures, bridge inspections and reopening decisions remain with the responsible authorities.
