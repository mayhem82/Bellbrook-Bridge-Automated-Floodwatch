Monitoring Network & Crossing Coverage Findings

Supporting Annexure to UMFM-SUB-001

Companion Evidence: CE-01, CE-02 and CE-04 · Discovery baseline: Snapshot 012.

1. Purpose and evidentiary rule

This annexure records the crossing-by-crossing monitoring findings underlying the Upper Macleay submission. It distinguishes direct monitoring, indirect or catchment relationships, known infrastructure whose crossing relationship requires proof, candidate local gaps, established controls and unresolved locations.

Control rule: station existence does not prove that a particular crossing is directly gauged. Failure to identify a dedicated public monitor in authoritative material examined is not proof that no unlisted sensor exists.

4. Monitoring taxonomy

Direct crossing monitoring: a gauge, camera or validated source is directly associated with the crossing.

Catchment / tributary station: a station exists in the relevant watercourse or catchment, but direct representation of a specific crossing is not assumed.

Indirect crossing relationship: official planning relates a crossing to another gauge or location rather than a dedicated local gauge.

Ungauged crossing within a monitored tributary: a station may exist in the tributary while the crossing itself remains described or treated as ungauged.

No dedicated public monitoring identified: no dedicated public monitor was identified in authoritative material examined; this is not proof that no unlisted asset exists.

UNKNOWN: evidence is insufficient to establish the monitoring relationship or failure mechanism.

5. Priority findings

  • Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek is the strongest verified warning-gauge deficiency because the official flood plan records no appropriate flood-warning gauge and identifies High Flood Island consequence.
  • Dowlings Falls combines repeated closure/damage with no dedicated public monitoring identified in the audited network.
  • Kogal demonstrates why station existence and crossing coverage must be separated: the station exists, but representation of the multiple Nulla Nulla Creek Road crossings remains to be established.
  • Bellbrook, Turners Flat, Sherwood and Georges Creek demonstrate that established monitoring controls can coexist with a separate access-restoration information gap.
  • Armidale Road demonstrates that a route can fail through landslip and road damage away from a monitored river crossing.
  • UNKNOWN classifications remain for Rossiters Road, McIntyres Road and Mackenzies Creek Road until evidence supports a stronger finding.

6. Requested evidence and implementation response

  1. Identify the monitoring source relied upon for every flood-critical crossing/corridor.
  2. State whether each relationship is direct, calibrated, indirect, ungauged or unresolved.
  3. Publish suitable existing data where available.
  4. Validate station-to-crossing relationships before duplicating hardware.
  5. Assess new gauges and/or cameras where genuine local coverage deficiency is established.
  6. Design monitoring and public-status capability into new critical access infrastructure.
  7. Integrate hydrological information with authoritative road, bridge, landslip, inspection and reopening status where corridor failure is multi-causal.
  8. Resolve outstanding monitoring records through GIPA-001 and related information requests.

7. Evidence control

Snapshot 012 is the frozen discovery baseline. Later evidence may append to or explicitly correct a classification, but earlier evidence is not silently deleted. The Official Documents library and Snapshot 001–012 chain provide traceability.
