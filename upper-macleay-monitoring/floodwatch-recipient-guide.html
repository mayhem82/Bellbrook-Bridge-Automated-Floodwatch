Bellbrook Floodwatch: what the webapp demonstrates

Independent community-built flood information interface — reusable explanatory document for public officials, agencies and community stakeholders

1. What you are being shown

Bellbrook Floodwatch is an independent web application that brings publicly available Bellbrook Bridge river-gauge information and flood-camera imagery into a mobile-accessible interface. It demonstrates how existing observations can be presented with recorded history and visual context instead of requiring a resident to interpret separate sources unaided.

Open the demonstration: Bellbrook Floodwatch webapp.

2. Why it was developed

For residents who depend on flood-affected crossings, a single reading does not explain the complete access situation. River height, the rate and direction of change, imagery, and official road or bridge status answer different questions. A usable public interface should make those distinctions clear and reduce the effort needed to locate and interpret available information.

3. What the recipient can inspect

  • River-level display: the gauge reading presented with an explicit water-level label and context relevant to Bellbrook Bridge.
  • Historical gauge record and trend: captured observations that support inspection of changes over time, rather than only changes while the page remains open.
  • Flood-camera view: publicly supplied bridge imagery, with a capture/archive mechanism for recorded frames.
  • Timelapse and visual presentation: archived camera frames can be viewed as a sequence; Cartoon Mode provides an alternative privacy-conscious presentation.
  • Mobile use: the interface is intended for access from a phone, reflecting how residents may obtain information during disrupted conditions.

These are design and repository-level functions. The availability and freshness of any individual reading, frame or interface function must be checked on the live site at the time of use; this document does not certify uninterrupted operation.

4. What the demonstration establishes

The application shows that a community developer can organise existing public observations into a clearer, historically useful interface. It is an example of a presentation and access layer, not proof that the underlying monitoring network is complete or that a reading at one gauge describes every upstream crossing.

5. What the demonstration does not establish

It does not independently determine whether a bridge is safe to cross, issue an official flood warning, authorise closure or reopening, substitute for a structural inspection, or guarantee data continuity. Water falling below a bridge deck does not establish that the road has been inspected, cleared or officially reopened. Camera images may be delayed or incomplete, and network outages can interrupt updates.

6. Why it matters to the wider submission

The accompanying Upper Macleay submission examines monitoring coverage, crossing-specific information gaps, public communication, and the separate process of restoring access after flooding. Bellbrook Floodwatch demonstrates one practical improvement: accessible integration of existing public information. Other problems require official data ownership, crossing-to-gauge validation, inspection procedures, road-status publication, infrastructure decisions and accountable operational authority.

7. Requested consideration

Review the working interface and, where relevant, the accompanying evidence and submission materials. Consider whether public agencies could provide authoritative, consistently timestamped data and clear crossing-specific access status so community-facing tools do not have to infer operational decisions. Evaluate genuine monitoring gaps separately from deficiencies in publishing or interpreting data already collected.

8. Independence and limitations

This is an independent civilian project, not a Kempsey Shire Council, NSW SES, Bureau of Meteorology or other government service. It is supplied for explanation and governance discussion, not as emergency advice. For warnings and crossing decisions, rely on current authoritative emergency and road information. The application is a developing demonstration and should be assessed on what is actually visible and functioning when inspected.
