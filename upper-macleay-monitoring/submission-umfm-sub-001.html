Upper Macleay Flood Monitoring and Access Restoration

Submission to Michael Kemp MP

Prepared by an ordinary civilian and Bellbrook resident acting as a constituent. Independent public-interest evidence and advocacy submission.

1. Executive submission

This submission seeks practical improvement to Upper Macleay flood monitoring, public access information and post-flood access restoration. Its purpose is better governance through better evidence, transparency, accountability and public administration.

The evidence does not support a blanket claim that every crossing needs new monitoring infrastructure. It supports a crossing-by-crossing approach: retain established controls, validate indirect relationships, expose suitable existing data, and add monitoring only where a genuine coverage deficiency is established.

2. Core findings

  • Willawarrin / Hickeys Creek: official flood planning identifies Willawarrin as a High Flood Island and records that no appropriate flood-warning gauge exists.
  • Nulla Nulla / Kogal: Kogal AWRC 206900 is an automatic station associated with Nulla Nulla Creek, but the evidence examined does not establish which separate road crossings it validly represents or whether equivalent public real-time information is available.
  • Dowlings Falls: historical records show repeated closure, repair and significant flood damage. No dedicated public monitoring was identified in the audited network.
  • Post-flood access: receding water is not the same as restored access. Council records show inspection and debris clearance can be required before reopening, with damage creating an additional repair pathway.

3. Structural harm identified

The companion evidence identifies a structural pathway rather than an allegation of individual wrongdoing:

fragmented monitoring/access information → incomplete situational picture → interpretation burden transferred to residents → increased ambiguity and information latency → potential delayed or incorrect access decisions → prolonged or poorly understood isolation → increased exposure to medical, supply and access consequences.

Twenty-eight structural-harm mechanisms are catalogued in UMFM-ANN-002 / CE-03, including source fragmentation, crossing-to-gauge ambiguity, public-access failure, multi-crossing corridor compression, information latency, medical-access isolation, post-flood decision queues and missing restoration-time metrics.

4. Access restoration is a separate operational phase

The evidence supports an explicit access-state model:

NORMAL → FLOOD THREAT → WATER OVER CROSSING / CLOSED → WATER RECEDING → WATER CLEAR BUT CLOSED → AWAITING INSPECTION → INSPECTED / AWAITING DEBRIS CLEARANCE → DAMAGE / REPAIR REQUIRED if applicable → ACCESS RESTORED → OPEN STATUS PUBLISHED.

The requested performance metric is therefore HYDROLOGICAL CLEARANCE → OFFICIAL REOPENING, with the intervening operational timestamps retained where available.

5. Evidence-based priority

Priority is based on flood/access exposure, consequence of loss of access, information/control weakness and medical/essential-service consequence. The strongest verified deficiencies are not treated as equivalent to locations where controls already exist. UNKNOWN is retained where evidence is insufficient.

UMFM-ANN-001 provides the controlled crossing and corridor register and preserves Snapshot 001–012 as the frozen discovery baseline.

6. Requested actions

  1. Establish the authoritative monitoring source and crossing relationship for each flood-critical Upper Macleay crossing and corridor.
  2. Validate whether existing gauges and stations directly represent the crossings for which residents may rely on them.
  3. Make suitable existing real-time monitoring information publicly accessible where it is operationally useful.
  4. Assess dedicated gauges and/or cameras where a genuine local coverage deficiency is established, prioritising consequence as well as flood frequency.
  5. Document the post-flood bridge inspection, debris-clearance, damage-escalation, engineering and reopening process in an SOP (Standard Operating Procedure), work instruction or equivalent controlled process.
  6. Record and publish access-restoration state separately from river-height or warning state.
  7. Measure hydrological-clearance-to-reopening latency and the intermediate decision timestamps.
  8. Clarify whether single-access communities, medical access, essential-service consequence and High Flood Island status affect inspection and restoration priority.
  9. Design monitoring, communications and public status into new critical-access infrastructure rather than treating them as later additions.
  10. Provide the records identified in the companion Information Requests register so unresolved questions can be converted into evidence-based findings.

7. Decision points requiring clarification

The operational chain separates monitoring, warning, closure, inspection allocation, inspection, debris clearance, engineering/damage escalation, repair, reopening authorisation and public OPEN publication. Evidence that one step occurred is not evidence that the next occurred.

The companion requests the responsible organisation and, where applicable, the delegated role, criteria and documentary record for each decision point. Where the evidence does not establish a role or delegation, the status remains UNKNOWN.

8. Information requests

The Information Requests register contains five controlled acquisition streams:

  1. GIPA-001: monitoring assets, station ownership and operational status.
  2. GIPA-002: post-flood bridge inspection and reopening procedure.
  3. GIPA-003: event-level inspection, clearance and reopening timestamps.
  4. GIPA-004: inspection prioritisation and isolated-community consequences.
  5. GIPA-005: Upper Macleay Flood Study Council decision record.

A request is not treated as evidence of wrongdoing and is not marked lodged until actual submission evidence exists.

10. Evidence and engagement standard

The work uses lawful, respectful, evidence-led engagement. Facts, inference, questions and unresolved matters are kept distinct. UNKNOWN remains UNKNOWN until evidence supports a finding. Contrary evidence and material corrections are retained transparently. Questions seek operational facts and records rather than admissions or predetermined culpability.

11. Requested response

The requested response is substantive engagement with the identified monitoring relationships, verified warning-gauge deficiency, access-restoration process, responsibility questions and outstanding records. Where a proposed control is unnecessary because an adequate existing control already operates, identifying and evidencing that control is a satisfactory form of resolution.

This is an independent community evidence and advocacy submission. It is not operated, endorsed or affiliated with Kempsey Shire Council, NSW State Emergency Service, Bureau of Meteorology, WaterNSW or another government authority. It is not an emergency-warning, evacuation, road-closure or safe-crossing service and is not legal, engineering, hydrological, medical or emergency-management advice.

Findings distinguish verified evidence, inference, unresolved questions and UNKNOWN. Failure to identify evidence is not proof that it does not exist. Agencies and affected parties may provide corrections, additional records and contrary evidence; substantive corrections are to be preserved transparently rather than silently rewriting the evidence history.
