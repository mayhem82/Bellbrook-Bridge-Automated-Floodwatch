Project mission

Bellbrook Bridge Automated Floodwatch

Independent community information tool developed by Julian Minko, a Bellbrook, NSW resident. Floodwatch makes available river observations easier to read on a phone. No agency endorsement is implied.

Why it was built

Residents need clear local information about river levels relevant to Bellbrook Bridge. Official observations, camera images, warnings and road-access decisions are spread across different services. Floodwatch brings available observations, recent trends and links to official sources into one local view.

What it does

The dashboard displays available river-height observations for Bellbrook station 059122, using the OpenDataSoft current and historical mirrors and retained observation history. It checks for available observations every five minutes. A successful check does not mean a new observation has arrived. Observation age and the last source-check time are displayed separately.

Charts, indicative clearance calculations and local water-level bands are generated by Floodwatch. The bands describe the recorded level relative to a deck reference; they are not official crossing status or an agency warning classification.

Bridge reference and indicative clearance

The calculation is 2.80 m reference minus recorded gauge height. It is not a direct measurement of current clearance beneath the bridge. The 2023 Macleay–Kempsey Catchment Resilience Project report, pages 66–67, records Bellbrook station 059122 with a 2.80 m bridge-deck reference, explicitly not metres AHD, and records the addition of bridge references to BOM hydrographs on 15 August 2023. This historical reference is not presented as a new engineering survey or confirmation of present bridge geometry, gauge datum or local water-surface conditions.

Why bridge-deck levels differ from flood classifications

The Bellbrook source table lists minor, moderate and major flood classifications of 6.50 m, 10.50 m and 13.50 m. The 6.50 m minor classification is 3.70 m above the app’s 2.80 m reference. The local reference and broader flood classifications serve different purposes. This comparison does not establish when warnings are issued or that warnings must wait for the minor level. Follow current BOM information and NSW SES advice.

Sources attribution and limitations

River-height observations: Bureau of Meteorology, © Commonwealth of Australia. The BOM Bellbrook table carries a Creative Commons Attribution notice; its licence link resolves to CC BY 4.0 International. The page also states that most river-height data comes from other agencies and is subject to supplying organisations’ terms. The licence applies to the material it covers, subject to those stated qualifications.

Delivery sources: OpenDataSoft current mirror and historical mirror. The current mirror’s licence and licence-URL metadata fields were blank when checked on 8 November 2026. Floodwatch does not assert a separate CC licence for the mirror. Its description identifies operational data that has not been quality controlled and notes that supplying-agency approval may be required for other uses.

Changes and presentation: Floodwatch selects and retains observations, plots charts, calculates indicative clearance and applies local water-level bands. These are independent presentations, not BOM-issued warnings. Original observation times are retained. All displayed observation times use AEST (UTC+10), which is one hour behind NSW daylight-saving time.

Camera imagery is a separate source from the river data. Archived time-lapse frames are processed images, not a live view or an unaltered official image. The BOM data licence is not asserted to cover Council camera imagery. See the Council flood-camera page for its source information.

Use and safety

Observations and camera images may be delayed, unavailable or out of date. They do not establish that a bridge or connecting road is open or safe. Floodwatch does not replace emergency warnings, authorised road-closure notices or inspections. Follow official warnings, closures and directions. Never enter floodwater.

Check each observation or image timestamp. An old observation alone does not identify whether the delay is at the station, in transmission, in publication or in the mirror. No accuracy, continuity or fitness for a particular purpose is guaranteed. This notice does not limit reuse rights granted under an applicable source licence.

Design standard

The interface is high-contrast and phone-first. Its main number is indicative clearance from a published reference. A low recorded level is not evidence of safe access; damage, debris and connecting-road conditions require separate official assessment.
